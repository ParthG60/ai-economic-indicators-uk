# UK AI Economic Indicators

A UK replication of the [Stanford Digital Economy Lab's AI Economic Indicators](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/indicators/),
built entirely from public UK data. It asks three questions and gives each its own track:

1. **Adoption** — how far AI use has spread across UK firms and people, and whether the public expects to gain from it.
2. **Transformation** — whether AI is yet visible in the wider economy as fast, capital-led growth, across nine macro indicators.
3. **Labour market** — whether AI is changing who gets hired, starting with young workers in the most exposed jobs. (After Stanford's "canaries" study.)

**▶ Live dashboard: [parthgoyal.uk/ai-economic-indicators-uk](https://parthgoyal.uk/ai-economic-indicators-uk/)**

The dashboard is static HTML with interactive [Plotly](https://plotly.com/python/) charts, generated by `src/build_dashboard.py`.

## What it finds

- **Adoption is climbing on every measure.** UK firm adoption has gone from roughly a tenth of businesses in 2023 to about a quarter now; just over half of adults say they have used an AI tool.
- **No clear sign of macro takeoff yet**, which matches what Stanford finds for the US. The one strong UK signal is the money spent importing cloud and compute. Core output and productivity look ordinary.
- **Hiring of young workers into the most AI-exposed jobs has dropped sharply** since ChatGPT, while the total headcount in those jobs has moved far less. That gap points to the hiring gate rather than layoffs. The important caveat: those jobs are also the most home-workable, so an AI effect and a remote-work effect cannot be cleanly separated. Read it as a warning sign, not proof.

The dashboard states these caveats in full on each page. This is descriptive measurement, not causal estimation.

## Data sources

All public: ONS (Business Insights survey, national accounts, productivity, trade, the Opinions and Lifestyle Survey, and **Labour Force Survey microdata** via the UK Data Service), the Bank of England, the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, and Ofcom. Occupational AI exposure is from the ILO's 2025 generative-AI index; automation and augmentation shares from the Anthropic Economic Index; cross-country firm adoption from Yotzov and co-authors (2026).

### The microdata is not in this repo

The labour-market track uses LFS person-level files, which are licensed to a single named user under the UK Data Service End User Licence and **must not be redistributed**. They are excluded here. What *is* committed is the set of **aggregated, indexed CSVs** the dashboard reads. Anyone with their own UKDS licence can regenerate them: see [`data/canaries/lfs/README.md`](data/canaries/lfs/README.md).

## Reproduce it

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python src/run_all.py        # regenerates every tracker CSV + the four dashboard pages
```

`run_all.py` runs the trackers in dependency order, then `build_dashboard.py`. The adoption and transformation tracks pull live from ONS / Bank of England / DESNZ (with curated fallbacks if a fetch fails); the labour-market track needs the LFS microdata in place first.

## Layout

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `src/` | Trackers (`adoption_monitor.py`, `takeoff_tracker.py`, `canaries_lfs_*.py`), the ONS fetch helper, and `build_dashboard.py`. |
| `data/` | Tidy CSV outputs by track. `data/canaries/lfs/` is where you place the (gitignored) microdata. |
| `dashboard/` | The built HTML pages (the same files served live on the website). |
| `reference/` | Exposure crosswalks and the design system note. Source PDFs are gitignored. |
| `PROJECT.md` / `STATUS.md` | The stable spec and the running log. |
| `BUILD_LOG.md` | How this was built, including the AI-assisted workflow. |
